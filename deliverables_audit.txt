007 [Front Matter Heading] Document Overview
008 [Normal] This Software Requirements Specification describes IliGuide, a web-based visitor reservation and destination-operations system for Tinago Falls, Mimbalot Falls, Timoga Cold Springs, and Sikyop Falls. It defines users, business context, functional scope, data, security boundaries, quality requirements, risks, costs, and verification criteria. The specification follows the 27-section structure of the Volere Requirements Specification Template, Version 16, and applies requirements-quality and traceability principles described in ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 (International Organization for Standardization et al., 2018; Robertson & Robertson, 2012).
009 [Normal] The document aligns proposed requirements with features visible in the IliGuide project repository. Repository evidence does not establish that every feature is deployed, operational at each destination, approved by the responsible authority, or evaluated with users. The specification distinguishes repository-supported behavior from proposed requirements and decisions that need confirmation. Physical capacity estimates are not treated as officially approved admission limits, and the system is not described as independently measuring ecological carrying capacity.

044 [Heading 1] 1. The Purpose of the Project
045 [Heading 2] 1a. The User Business or Background of the Project Effort
046 [Normal] Iligan City has several nature-based tourism destinations with different admission procedures, operating conditions, visitor services, and management arrangements. The four destinations covered by this specification are Tinago Falls, Mimbalot Falls, Timoga Cold Springs, and Sikyop Falls. Dodiongan Falls is outside the defined system scope. Visitor management across the selected sites requires staff to know which visits are expected, which visitors have arrived, and which visitors remain onsite. Admission records alone cannot provide a current occupancy count when departures are not recorded consistently.
047 [Normal] The initial requirements material describes manual registration and variable visitor-counting practices. The confirmed site corrections are that Mimbalot Falls has no entrance fee, Sikyop Falls charges entrance and guide fees, and the Timoga operation uses a logbook while staff sometimes count visitors. These practices differ by destination and should not be represented as one uniform procedure. Current fee amounts, written site policies, and the authority responsible for approving them must be confirmed before each destination is configured.
048 [Normal] The City Tourism Office has an existing Tourism Management Information System (TMIS). Available project notes indicate that tourism statistics are entered or reviewed through existing procedures, but the extent of TMIS coverage for reservations, ticketing, departures, and live occupancy has not been established. IliGuide is not specified to integrate with TMIS. Its purpose is to support destination-level visitor operations without claiming to replace or duplicate TMIS functions that have not been examined.
049 [Normal] IliGuide provides public destination information and reservations, visitor tickets, staff-assisted walk-in registration, QR-based attendance recording, destination schedules, and operational views of visitor activity. The system can support staff decisions by presenting recorded counts and configured limits. It cannot make an ecological, safety, or legal determination from those records alone. Tourism carrying capacity is a management concept that requires clear objectives and context, rather than a universal number calculated from visitor counts alone (McCool & Lime, 2001).
050 [Heading 2] 1b. Goals of the Project
051 [Normal] The project’s principal goal is to develop a shared visitor-management system that organizes reservations, ticket validation, visitor registration, attendance records, destination schedules, and site-specific operational information for the four selected destinations. Visitors should be able to find current destination details, select an available visit period, provide booking information, and retrieve an eligible ticket. Staff should be able to record admissions and departures, review reservations, and administer only destinations within their authority.
052 [Normal] The project also aims to make visitor counts more useful for daily operations. A reservation represents an expected visit, while an attendance event represents a recorded arrival or departure. IliGuide keeps those concepts separate and derives current occupancy from recorded attendance. This enables an operational view of expected visitors, arrivals, departures, and visitors currently inside, subject to staff recording accuracy and network availability.
053 [Normal] The system supports two distinct forms of capacity information. Physical assessments and mapped zones can store measurements and estimates, while approved visitor limits and booking-slot capacity remain separately configured operational controls. A physical estimate does not automatically create an approved limit. The project has not established ecological carrying capacity for the destinations or identified official approved limits for all sites.
054 [Normal] Project evaluation should verify reservation calculations, capacity enforcement, ticket validation, attendance state changes, role-based access, and the clarity of visitor and staff workflows. Usability and performance thresholds that require participant results or stakeholder approval remain targets to be established; they are not reported as completed evaluation outcomes.
055 [Normal] The goals used for requirements traceability are summarized below. They describe the intended outcomes of the system and do not claim that a destination has approved a policy or that a feature has completed user evaluation.

081 [Heading 2] 3a. Solution Constraints
082 [Normal] IliGuide is a responsive web application built with React and TypeScript and connected to Supabase. Destination records, bookings, attendance, roles, and operational settings are read from the configured database rather than supplied as permanent local sample data. Database and Storage access depend on authentication and row-level policies. Users need an internet connection for database-backed reservations and staff actions. The application shell may be available offline as a progressive web application, but this does not provide offline booking or attendance synchronization.
083 [Normal] The application must preserve destination-specific fees, schedules, management authorities, and visitor limits. System estimates must remain separate from official limits. The current design supports manual/demo GCash and Maya payment records and onsite payment workflows; the demo flow explicitly states that it does not transfer money.

Supabase provides authentication, PostgreSQL data storage, Row Level Security, Storage, Realtime updates, and Edge Functions. The project also uses a service worker for app-shell caching and includes configuration for Vercel hosting.
086 [Normal] These technologies describe the source repository, not a verified production environment. Package versions and deployment settings can change and must be rechecked before release. 124 [Heading 2] 6b. The Context of the Work
125 [Normal] Visitors interact with the public portal to learn about destinations and reserve visits. Gatekeepers interact with assigned-site tools to manage admissions and attendance. Destination managers configure site data and staff within their organizational boundaries. The Tourism Office oversees only its assigned authority, while private-site administration remains separate. Supabase provides the application’s backend services and enforces database permissions.
126 [Normal] The work context includes internet connectivity, staff devices, camera access for QR scanning, destination site rules, and manually supplied site measurements. The City Treasurer’s Office may be involved in financial records, but its role in the current application has not been confirmed. TMIS is an external operating system, not an integrated IliGuide component.
127 [Heading 2] 6c. Work Partitioning
128 [Normal] The visitor-management work is partitioned by business events. Each event produces or updates records that support the next step in the visit process.
130 [Normal] The daily dashboard separates four measures that answer different operational questions. Expected visitors count participants on valid online reservations for the selected site and date, whether or not they have arrived. Arrivals count visitors whose entry is confirmed by QR scan during the Asia/Manila operational date, including walk-ins after their ticket is scanned and confirmed. Departures count visitors whose exit is confirmed during that date. Currently Inside is the sum of visitor counts on active QR-confirmed attendance records and persists across midnight until a confirmed exit changes the records. Creating a reservation or walk-in booking, or looking up its reference manually, does not increase the occupancy value. These values are estimates of recorded activity, not a sensor-based count of everyone physically present.
131 [Heading 2] 6d. Specifying a Business Use Case
132 [Normal] The reservation business use case begins when a visitor selects a published destination and date. IliGuide retrieves open slots and available capacity, then collects visitor counts, individual visitor information, and a booking contact. It validates that fee information is confirmed, the party is within the configured limit, and any PWD count matches visitor-level claims. The system then confirms a free booking, creates an onsite-payment reservation, or opens the configured demo-payment flow. An eligible ticket becomes available only after the required status conditions are satisfied.
133 [Normal] The admission business use case begins when a visitor presents a QR ticket at the destination. An assigned Gatekeeper validates the booking, destination, visit date, payment eligibility, and attendance state. The first valid scan requests confirmation of actual entry; the system records check-in only after the Gatekeeper confirms. A later valid scan indicates exit and requests confirmation; the system records checkout only after confirmation. Duplicate, expired, unpaid, wrong-destination, or out-of-window actions must not be recorded as successful attendance. Manual booking lookup may help locate reservation details but must not change attendance or occupancy.
134 [Normal] The staff dashboard derives expected visitors from online reservations for the selected operational date and derives arrivals and departures from QR attendance events that the Gatekeeper confirms. A walk-in becomes an arrival only after its ticket is scanned and entry is confirmed. Current occupancy uses the total visitor count associated with active QR-confirmed attendance and is not reset at midnight. The dashboard’s daily arrival and departure totals use Asia/Manila date boundaries. A reservation remains an expected visit even after check-in, while its attendance event separately contributes to arrivals. Manual booking lookup may assist staff but must not change attendance or occupancy.
135 [Normal] The walk-in business use case begins when an assigned Gatekeeper records a visitor who arrived without an online reservation. The Gatekeeper selects an available slot, provides the booking contact and party details, and records the selected payment method. The database validates site assignment, slot availability, capacity, and visitor limits before it creates the walk-in record.
136 [Normal] The destination-administration business use case begins when a Site Admin or Tourism Office user changes information for an assigned site. IliGuide checks user organization and site authority before allowing the change. The application stores schedules, closures, fee settings, duty assignments, announcements, and visitor-limit records in the authorized site scope.
137 [Heading 1] 7. Business Data Model and Data Dictionary
138 [Heading 2] 7a. Business Data Model
139 [Normal] The business model contains destination, user, reservation, attendance, configuration, and communication records. A destination belongs to a management organization. Staff assignments connect users to roles and destinations. A reservation belongs to one visitor account or authorized booking contact, one destination, and one selected slot. Its visitor records describe the people included in the reservation. Payments and PWD verification records are associated with the reservation and its eligible visitor records. Attendance records describe entry and exit events. Capacity assessments and map zones are attached to a destination but do not automatically determine approved limits.
140 [Normal] The conceptual model deliberately separates expected visits from actual visits. It also separates physical estimates from slot capacity and approved visitor limits. This prevents a mathematical estimate or booking total from being misinterpreted as a live occupancy count or official limit. The physical database contains additional technical records for holds, access tokens, notifications, audit events, and organization membership.

147 [Heading 2] 8b. Product Use Case Table
149 [Heading 2] 8c. Individual Product Use Cases
150 [Normal] For UC-01, a visitor opens the catalogue or destination page. IliGuide displays only published destination information and identifies when fee information has not been confirmed. The visitor can view location, site details, relevant schedules, reviews, and current advisories. The system does not promise that an unverified map boundary or fee is authoritative.
151 [Normal] For UC-03, the visitor selects a date and available slot, enters the party count and contact information, and reviews the fee calculation. The system validates slot status and capacity at the database boundary. If the total is zero, the free reservation is confirmed without payment. A payable booking follows the site’s configured onsite or demo workflow. A failed payment submission does not become a confirmed payment.
152 [Normal] For UC-04, each visitor claiming a configured PWD discount must have a matching visitor record, a category, and an image in the private PWD storage bucket. An upload is not itself a verification decision. Authorized site management or an assigned Gatekeeper reviews the claim. The current migration specifies a deletion date 30 days after the visit date, which must be formally validated before real use.
153 [Normal] For UC-05 and UC-06, an eligible visitor retrieves a signed ticket and presents it to a Gatekeeper. The first valid QR scan presents the booking for entry confirmation. After the Gatekeeper confirms actual entry, IliGuide records attendance and updates current occupancy. A later scan of the active visit presents an exit confirmation; current occupancy decreases and the departure is recorded only after the Gatekeeper confirms checkout. Manual booking lookup may help locate reservation details but cannot substitute for QR confirmation or change attendance. The stored state, not a simple count of reservations, drives current occupancy.
154 [Normal] For UC-07, an assigned Gatekeeper selects an available slot, enters contact and party details, and records the payment method. The system validates the booking and configured limits before it creates the walk-in record. It then presents a booking reference and, when ticket issuance succeeds, a QR ticket. Registration alone does not count the walk-in as an arrival or as currently inside; the Gatekeeper must scan the QR ticket and confirm entry.
155 [Normal] For UC-08 and UC-09, a manager updates only destinations in the manager’s authority. The manager can configure site information, schedules, closures, fees, staff assignments, and operational limits as permitted. Authorized staff can review live attendance and map data. Physical estimates remain informational unless an authorized limit is separately entered and approved.

165 [Heading 1] 11. Usability and Humanity Requirements

The Gatekeeper workflow should keep scanning, manual lookup, walk-in registration, and exit recording distinct and provide a visible result for each action. 168 [Heading 2] 11b. Personalization and Internationalization
169 [Normal] The current interface is primarily in English and formats money in Philippine pesos. Operational reporting uses the Asia/Manila time zone. No additional language option or localization requirement has been approved. Future language support should preserve the meaning of fee, payment, safety, privacy, and ticket statuses.
170 [Heading 2] 11c. Learning Requirements
171 [Normal] Visitors should be able to complete account registration, destination discovery, and reservation using in-interface labels and guidance. Gatekeepers and administrators require short task-based instructions for scanning, walk-ins, attendance, schedules, capacity records, and review decisions. The system should not rely on staff remembering undocumented status transitions.
172 [Heading 2] 11d. Understandability and Politeness
173 [Normal] The system should use consistent terms for booking, payment, entry, exit, occupancy, and capacity. Error messages should identify the next useful action without exposing database errors or blaming the user. When data is unavailable, the system should state that it cannot confirm the result rather than showing a stale value as current.
174 [Heading 2] 11e. Accessibility Requirements
175 [Normal] Core workflows should be designed and evaluated against WCAG 2.2, with keyboard operation, programmatic labels, readable contrast, visible focus, text alternatives, and accessible error feedback as initial acceptance areas (World Wide Web Consortium, 2023). The repository has not been audited for WCAG conformance, so no conformance claim is made. Evaluation should document issues found and the level actually achieved.
176 [Heading 1] 12. Performance Requirements
177 [Heading 2] 12a. Speed and Latency Requirements
178 [Normal] No client-approved response-time threshold or load profile is present in the source material. Before production acceptance, the responsible stakeholder should set numeric targets for destination search, availability checks, booking submission, QR validation, and staff dashboard refresh. A performance test should record its environment, request volume, and response-time distribution for comparison with the approved target. 179 [Heading 2] 12b. Safety-Critical Requirements
180 [Normal] IliGuide is not a life-safety, rescue, or emergency dispatch system. It must not replace Gatekeeper judgment, site safety procedures, weather-related closure decisions, or emergency response. When an approved visitor limit is set, new admissions must be rejected at the configured threshold under the database rule. Staff remain responsible for physical conditions the system cannot observe.
181 [Heading 2] 12c. Precision or Accuracy Requirements
182 [Normal] Fee and discount calculations must use destination settings and round to currency precision. Attendance counts must use accepted entry and exit events. Physical-capacity estimates must display method, assumptions, date, and source where available. They must not be described as measurements of ecological sustainability unless a qualified assessment supports that interpretation.
183 [Heading 2] 12d. Reliability and Availability
184 [Normal] Reservations and attendance depend on Supabase and internet access. The project does not specify an uptime commitment, backup recovery objective, or disaster-recovery time. If a service request fails, the application must not present the requested change as saved. The operational owner must determine availability, backup, and recovery targets before production use.
185 [Heading 2] 12e. Robustness or Fault-Tolerance
186 [Normal] The application should reject duplicate, expired, invalid, or inconsistent booking and attendance actions without corrupting booking state. Failed uploads or database requests should produce an understandable retry instruction. Staff may consult a paper record or use manual lookup during an outage, but neither action may change digital attendance or occupancy without a later confirmed QR scan. App-shell caching does not establish offline data capture or synchronization, so offline actions must not be represented as recorded.
187 [Heading 2] 12f. Capacity Requirements
188 [Normal] Reservation availability is controlled by destination settings, date-specific slot capacity, party-size limits, and separately configured approved visitor limits. The system should apply those values at the database layer so concurrent reservations cannot exceed configured limits. Physical area estimates remain informational and separate.
189 [Heading 2] 12g. Scalability or Extensibility
190 [Normal] Destination configuration should allow additional sites without hardcoding their fees, schedules, ownership, or visitor limits into public pages. The current database uses site records and organization-scoped permissions. Performance under additional destinations and concurrent users has not been measured and must be evaluated before expanding beyond the current four-site scope.
191 [Heading 2] 12h. Longevity
192 [Normal] Continued operation depends on maintaining browser compatibility, package versions, Supabase functions, database migrations, secrets, and external email or payment configurations. No service lifespan or maintenance contract has been approved. A designated technical owner should review dependency updates, backup status, security changes, and service credentials at an agreed interval.

195 [Normal] Visitors may use personal smartphones or computers. Gatekeepers require an internet-connected device with a camera or a supported manual lookup method. Device ruggedness, power supply, mobile-data provider, and site Wi-Fi are unspecified and must be assessed at each destination.

Secrets must not be stored in browser-visible variables. The repository includes deployment instructions, but production readiness and current deployment state have not been verified.

205 [Heading 2] 14b. Supportability Requirements

The system should provide enough diagnostic status without logging passwords, payment secrets, or private ID image contents. The responsible support organization has not yet been assigned.
207 [Heading 2] 14c. Adaptability Requirements
208 [Normal] Destination information, operating hours, slot capacity, closures, fees, management organization, and approved limits should remain configurable as data. Changes to role meanings, PWD verification, payment transitions, or attendance logic require review because they affect access, financial status, and occupancy reporting.
209 [Heading 1] 15. Security Requirements
210 [Heading 2] 15a. Access Requirements
211 [Normal] IliGuide shall identify visitors, Gatekeepers, Tourism Office personnel, and Site Admin users through authenticated accounts and role-based authorization. Staff access shall be limited by role, management organization, assigned destination, and permitted operation. Public registration shall not grant staff privileges. A Gatekeeper shall not access operational records for an unassigned site, and a Site Admin shall not access Tourism Office data or another private organization’s data. Supabase Row Level Security policies and server-side authorization checks shall enforce these boundaries; hiding a control in the interface alone is insufficient. Acceptance requires positive and negative authorization tests for every role and site boundary (Supabase, n.d.-a).
212 [Heading 2] 15b. Integrity Requirements
213 [Normal] The system shall preserve consistent relationships among bookings, participants, payment states, tickets, and attendance events. Booking creation and capacity allocation shall be validated at the database boundary to prevent simultaneous requests from exceeding configured slot capacity. Attendance shall follow a valid entry and exit state transition, and a failed database operation shall not be shown as successful. Administrative changes shall preserve the actor, destination scope, and relevant timestamps where audit records are supported. Migration review and backup procedures must be established before production data is relied upon.
214 [Heading 2] 15c. Privacy Requirements
215 [Normal] IliGuide collects personal information needed for accounts, reservations, visitor admission, and payment-state handling. PWD claims may include disability-related sensitive personal information and an image of an identification document. These records require a clearly stated purpose, appropriate notice and lawful basis, restricted access, secure handling, and an approved retention and deletion policy under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173, 2012). The current code specifies removal 30 days after the visit date, but that value is not a confirmed retention policy. The designated personal information controller and privacy officer must confirm necessity, notice, retention, deletion, and data-subject request procedures before real ID images are collected.
216 [Normal] PWD images shall remain in private storage and shall not be exposed through public destination pages, public URLs, analytics, or ordinary logs. Access shall be limited to the visitor and staff authorized to review the associated booking within its destination scope. Payment proof and contact data shall receive comparable access controls. The system shall not store card credentials in its demo payment flow. The repository’s configured controls require security review in the deployment environment before they are treated as production safeguards (Supabase, n.d.-b).
217 [Heading 2] 15d. Audit Requirements
218 [Normal] The system shall record selected security-sensitive administrative actions, including changes to destination configuration, staff assignment, payment review, and PWD verification where the current audit design supports the action. QR-confirmed attendance records shall identify the authenticated Gatekeeper, destination, action, time, and affected booking without copying passwords, secrets, full payment credentials, or ID image contents. Manual lookup shall not create an attendance event. Retention, access, export, and monitoring responsibilities for audit records remain to be approved by the system owner.
219 [Heading 2] 15e. Immunity Requirements
220 [Normal] IliGuide shall validate user input on both the interface and trusted server or database layer, restrict file uploads by size and accepted media type, and store private evidence outside public access. The service shall use parameterized database access and enforce authorization through policies rather than relying on user-supplied role labels. Dependencies, secrets, deployment configuration, and database policies require review before a production release. The project has not undergone an independent penetration test or security certification, so this specification makes no claim of immunity from attack. File upload safeguards should follow recognized security guidance, including extension and content validation, least privilege, and storage isolation (OWASP Foundation, n.d.).
221 [Heading 1] 16. Cultural Requirements
222 [Heading 2] 16a. Cultural Requirements
223 [Normal] IliGuide serves visitors and staff in Iligan City and must present destination names, local rules, fees, and safety guidance respectfully and consistently. The interface currently uses English and Philippine peso formatting. It should avoid stereotypes, informal labels for disability, and language that implies visitors are at fault when a system action fails. The project has not confirmed requirements for Cebuano, Filipino, or other language options. Any translation should be reviewed by a competent local speaker and preserve the meaning of safety, fee, privacy, and admission instructions. Destination-specific practices should be configured separately rather than forced into a uniform workflow where the site rules differ.
224 [Heading 1] 17. Legal Requirements
225 [Heading 2] 17a. Compliance Requirements
226 [Normal] The system owner must determine and document the lawful basis, privacy notices, access controls, retention periods, and procedures applicable to the personal information processed by IliGuide. Republic Act No. 10173 and its implementing rules govern personal data handling in the Philippines. Disability-related claims, identification images, contact details, and visitor histories require particular care because inappropriate disclosure could harm visitors. Applicable obligations under disability rights and identification rules must be reviewed with the responsible legal or privacy authority before the system relies on PWD evidence for admission or discounts (Republic Act No. 10754, 2016; National Council on Disability Affairs, 2026).

A simulated payment record is not proof that legal payment, tax, accounting, or receipt obligations have been met. The current SRS does not establish that those obligations have been reviewed by counsel or by the participating destination authorities.

240 [Heading 1] 20. New Problems
241 [Heading 2] 20a. Effects on the Current Environment
242 [Normal] Introducing IliGuide changes admission work from paper-centered recording toward account-based reservations and digital attendance. Staff may need to perform both manual and digital recording during an initial operational evaluation. The transition can improve retrieval and consistency only when staff record entries and departures promptly and the site has reliable access to the system. Local safety briefings, equipment issuance, receipts, and physical gate procedures remain part of site operations unless the responsible authority changes them.
243 [Heading 2] 20b. Effects on Installed Systems
244 [Normal] The current scope does not integrate with TMIS, Smart Pay, or the City Treasurer’s systems. Staff may therefore continue existing reports and financial reconciliation outside IliGuide. The repository includes code for certain external services, but their production deployment and relationship to visitor workflows are not verified. Before release, the technical owner must inventory enabled services, destinations, accounts, database migrations, and production secrets to avoid presenting a development configuration as an installed operational system.
245 [Heading 2] 20c. Potential User Problems
246 [Normal] Visitors may have limited connectivity, a shared device, difficulty uploading evidence, or trouble retrieving a ticket. Gatekeepers may encounter an unreadable QR code, a camera failure, or an unavailable database. Administrators may misconfigure fees, time slots, authority, or capacity. The interface should explain the status and an available next step, but staff still need a documented paper procedure for service interruptions. A paper record does not change digital occupancy; the digital count changes only after a valid QR check-in or confirmed QR checkout. Demo payment wording must remain clear so visitors do not mistake a simulated transaction for a real charge or confirmed settlement.
247 [Heading 2] 20d. Limitations in the Anticipated Environment
248 [Normal] The system cannot observe a physical visitor who was not recorded, confirm that every departure was entered, or determine changing environmental conditions through sensors. It does not provide offline booking or attendance synchronization, emergency dispatch, or automated ecological assessment. Map precision depends on supplied geometry and survey quality. These limitations constrain how occupancy, capacity, and map information may be used in operational decisions.
249 [Heading 2] 20e. Follow-Up Problems
250 [Normal] Post-release operation may require correction of destination information, access reviews, privacy requests, dependency updates, staff turnover, backup restoration, and support for devices or connectivity. As usage grows, the project may need formal monitoring, service agreements, accessibility evaluation, and additional performance testing. These are operational needs to plan and assign; the current project evidence does not show that service ownership or service-level commitments have been established.

A phased pilot can compare digital attendance with the destination’s approved operational records before broader adoption. These are solution ideas for consideration and do not change the current system scope.
