0106 [normal] Requirements Types
0107 [normal] For ease of use, we have found it convenient to think of requirements as belonging to a type. There are two reasons for the type: as an aid to finding the requirements, to be able to group the requirements that are relevant to a specific expert specialty.
0108 [normal] Functional requirements are the fundamental or essential subject matter of the product. They describe what the product has to do or what processing actions it must take.
0109 [normal] Copyright © the Atlantic Systems Guild Limited Volere Template V16 /3
0110 [normal] Non-functional requirements are the properties that the functions must have, such as performance and usability. Do not be deterred by the unfortunate name for this kind of requirements, they are as important as the functional requirements for the product’s success.
0111 [normal] Project constraints are restrictions on the product due to the budget or the time available to build the product.
0112 [normal] Design constraints impose restrictions on how the product must be designed. For example, it might have to be implemented in the hand-held device being given to major customers, or it might have to use the existing servers and desktop computers, or any other hardware, software, or business practice.
0113 [normal] Project drivers are the business-related forces. For example, the purpose of the project is a project driver, as are all of the stakeholders—each for different reasons.
0114 [normal] Project issues define the conditions under which the project will be done. Our reason for including them as part of the requirements is to present a coherent picture of all factors that contribute to the success or failure of the project and to illustrate how managers can use requirements as input when managing a project.
0115 [normal] Testing Requirements
0116 [normal] The Volere philosophy is to start testing requirements as soon as you start writing them. You make a requirement testable by adding its fit criterion. This fit criterion measures the requirement, making it possible to determine whether a given solution fits the requirement. If a fit criterion cannot be found for a requirement, then the requirement is either ambiguous or poorly understood. All requirements can be measured, and all should carry a fit criterion.

0118 [normal] The requirements shell is a guide to writing each atomic requirement. The components of the shell (also called a “snow card”) are identified below. For example: products that implement this requirement, version of the software that implements this requirement, departments who are interested in this requirement, etc. There are others but do not capriciously add attributes unless they really help you: every attribute you add needs to be maintained.

0122 [normal] 1. The Purpose of the Project
0124 [normal] 1a. The User Business or Background of the Project Effort
0125 [normal] Iligan City has several nature-based tourist destinations, including Tinago Falls, Sikyop Underground cave, Mimbalot Falls, Timoga Springs, and etc. These places attract visitors, making visitor management important for maintaining organized site operations, ensuring visitor safety, and protecting the natural environment.
0126 [normal] Managing visitors requires knowing how many people are expected to visit, how many have actually arrived, and how many remain at a destination. This information helps site personnel manage admissions and monitor visitor activity. However, recording entrance transactions alone may not provide a clear picture of the number of people currently inside if visitor departures are not also recorded.
0127 [normal] Based on our preliminary observation at Tinago Falls and Timoga Springs, visitors pay the entrance fee, receive a receipt, and provide their information in a physical logbook. While Mimbalot Falls and Sikyop Underground Cave do not require an entrance fee, these sites still need the visitor to record their information in a logbook manually. This provides a record of visitor entry, but we have yet to verify how departures are recorded and how the site uses these records to monitor visitor activity. The existing process also raises the question of how reservations, time-slot scheduling, and visitor records are managed across the other destinations.
0128 [normal] Iligan City also has an existing Tourism Management Information System (TMIS). However, its coverage of site-level reservations, visitor arrivals and departures, and capacity monitoring has not yet been established. Understanding its existing functions is important to determine how the proposed system can support current tourism management activities without unnecessarily duplicating them.
0129 [normal] In response to these concerns, this project proposes a shared mobile- and web-based e-ticketing and visitor scheduling system for participating nature-based tourist destinations in Iligan City. The system aims to provide a more organized way of handling reservations, ticket validation, visitor registration, and attendance records. It will also give authorized personnel access to visitor information and reports that can support site operations and planning.
0130 [normal] Visitors will be able to view destination information, check available schedules, and book their visits online. Those without online reservations can still be accommodated through onsite registration, where authorized personnel will register them and issue printed tickets.
0131 [normal] Each participating destination will also be able to manage its own schedules and visitor limits based on available assessments and limits approved by the responsible authorities. This is important because the destinations may have different conditions and visitor-management requirements. The system will support the implementation of these limits but will not independently determine the ecological carrying capacity of each destination. Form

0152 [normal] 1b. Goals of the Project
0153 [normal] The main goal of this project is to develop a shared e-ticketing and visitor scheduling system that will help manage and monitor visitors at participating nature-based tourist destinations in Iligan City.
0154 [normal] Specifically, the project aims to:
0155 [normal] Provide a common platform for reservations and ticketing. Allow visitors to view destinations, check available schedules, book visits, and receive digital tickets. The system will also support walk-in registration.
0156 [normal] Improve visitor attendance recording. Allow authorized personnel to validate tickets and record visitor arrivals and departures where applicable. This will provide more organized attendance records for monitoring visitor activity.
0157 [normal] Manage schedules and visitor limits for each destination. Allow site personnel to set available time slots and approved visitor limits based on the needs and conditions of their respective destinations.
0158 [normal] Provide destination information and mapping. Give visitors access to important details such as entrance fees, operating hours, safety guidelines, site advisories, and the locations of participating tourist destinations through a map.
0159 [normal] Provide capacity mapping for participating destinations. Present the identified usable visitor areas of each destination and their corresponding physical carrying-capacity estimates. This will help authorized personnel understand how visitor limits are distributed within the site and support scheduling and visitor management decisions.
0160 [normal] Generate visitor attendance reports. Help authorized personnel review reservations, walk-in admissions, arrivals, departures, and visitor trends through organized reports.
0162 [Heading 3] Purpose
0163 [normal] To develop a shared web-based e-ticketing and visitor scheduling system with physical carrying capacity assessment and mapping features for participating nature-based tourist destinations in Iligan City. The system aims to support visitor reservations, admission, attendance monitoring, and site-specific scheduling while providing information that can assist in managing visitor activity.
0164 [Heading 3] Advantage
0165 [normal] The proposed system will provide a more organized way to manage visitor reservations, ticketing, and admission across participating destinations. By recording visitor arrivals and departures, it will help authorized personnel monitor attendance and estimate the number of visitors currently inside each destination.
0166 [normal] The system will also provide destination mapping and physical capacity mapping to help personnel identify usable visitor areas and review their estimated physical carrying capacities. These estimates may support the development of appropriate visitor schedules and admission limits, subject to review and approval by the responsible authorities. Visitors will also benefit from easier access to destination information, available schedules, and online reservations.
0167 [Heading 3] Measurement
0168 [normal] The system will be evaluated through functional testing, calculation verification, and usability evaluation involving its intended users. Testing will determine whether reservations, digital ticket issuance and validation, walk-in registration, arrival and departure recording, scheduling, destination mapping, capacity mapping, and report generation function according to the specified requirements.
0169 [normal] The accuracy of attendance and occupancy records will be checked against prepared test records. Physical carrying capacity calculations will be verified against independently computed reference values using the same measurements and assumptions. Scheduling functions will also be tested to determine whether they correctly enforce configured visitor limits when such limits are available.
0170 [normal] Usability evaluation will gather feedback from authorized City Tourism Office personnel, site gatekeepers, and visitors regarding the system's ease of use, clarity, and suitability for their respective activities.
0171 [Heading 3] Expected Project Outcome
0172 [normal] The expected output is a working web-based application prototype consisting of four main components:
0173 [normal] Public Portal – Provides destination information, location mapping, available schedules, online reservations, and digital tickets for visitors.
0174 [normal] Gatekeeper Module – Supports ticket validation, walk-in registration, visitor arrival and departure recording, and attendance monitoring at participating destinations.
0175 [normal] Management Dashboard – Provides visitor attendance records, reservation statistics, reports, and physical capacity mapping to help authorized personnel review visitor activity and site conditions.
0176 [normal] System Administration Module – Supports user account management, destination configuration, scheduling rules, and the management of authorized visitor limits and system settings.
0177 [normal] The system will incorporate physical carrying capacity calculations based on usable visitor areas, space allocation per visitor, operating hours, and average visit duration, provided that the necessary data can be established. These calculations will produce estimates that may be used to support scheduling and visitor-management decisions.
0178 [normal] The system will be evaluated based on its functionality, calculation and data accuracy, and usability. It will not independently establish official ecological carrying capacity limits, process online payments, or integrate with the existing Tourism Management Information System (TMIS).

0211 [normal] 2c. Other Stakeholders
0212 [normal] Content
0213 [normal] Aside from the Iligan City Tourism Office, which serves as the client and customer of the proposed system, the other identified stakeholders are the site gatekeepers, visitors or tourists, the City Treasurer's Office, and the project development team. These stakeholders are either directly affected by the proposed system or provide knowledge necessary for defining its requirements.
0214 [normal] The site gatekeepers are personnel under the Iligan City Tourism Office and are the persons directly responsible for the day-to-day operation of their respective tourism sites. They report to the City Tourism Office and serve as the primary operational users of the proposed system. Their knowledge is needed to understand the actual visitor admission process, walk-in transactions, entrance-fee collection, visitor recording, ticket validation, site rules, operating procedures, and other activities that take place at the site level. Their involvement in the project is considered high because the proposed system will directly affect their daily work. Their influence is also high for operational requirements, particularly those involving visitor registration, ticket validation, arrival and departure recording, and site-level visitor management.
0215 [normal] The visitors or tourists are external stakeholders and end users of the public-facing part of the proposed system. They will interact with functions such as viewing destination information, checking schedules, making reservations, and obtaining tickets. Their input is needed to determine requirements related to usability, accessibility, clarity of information, reservation procedures, and the overall ease of using the system. Their involvement is considered medium to high, particularly during requirements gathering and usability evaluation. Their influence is high for public-facing and usability requirements, but lower for administrative and operational policies that are determined by the City Tourism Office and site gatekeepers.
0216 [normal] The City Treasurer's Office is a financial stakeholder because monetary collections from the tourism sites fall under the City Treasurer. Its knowledge is relevant to requirements involving entrance-fee records, transaction summaries, financial accountability, and other information related to collections that may be recorded or reported by the proposed system. Its involvement is considered medium because the Treasurer's Office is not expected to participate in daily visitor admission or reservation activities. However, its influence is high for requirements involving financial records and collection-related information. The proposed system will not assume responsibility for online payment processing unless this is included in a future scope.
0217 [normal] The project development team is responsible for gathering and analyzing requirements, designing the system, developing its features, conducting testing, and documenting the project. The team requires accurate information from the City Tourism Office, site gatekeepers, and visitors in order to translate organizational and operational needs into system requirements. Its involvement is high throughout the project, and its influence is high in technical design, implementation, testing, and feasibility decisions. However, the development team will not independently determine tourism policies, financial procedures, or operational rules that fall under the authority of the responsible stakeholders.
0218 [normal] Motivation
0219 [normal] Identifying these stakeholders is necessary because each group contributes different knowledge needed for the development of the proposed system. The site gatekeepers provide knowledge about actual site operations, visitors provide the end-user perspective, the City Treasurer's Office provides knowledge related to monetary collections and financial records, and the project development team provides the technical knowledge needed to design and implement the system. Failure to consider these stakeholders may result in incomplete requirements, impractical system functions, or features that do not reflect the actual responsibilities of the people involved.

The proposed system should therefore provide straightforward navigation, understandable reports, and clearly labeled management functions.
0236 [normal] Other User Characteristics: These personnel perform office-based responsibilities and may need to review information from multiple tourism destinations. 0238 [normal] User Category: Site Gatekeepers
0239 [normal] User Role: Site gatekeepers are responsible for the daily visitor-related operations of their assigned destinations. They will also assist visitors and follow site-specific admission procedures.
0240 [normal] Subject Matter Experience: Journeyman (provisional). The interviews indicate that site gatekeepers have direct knowledge of visitor admission procedures, operating conditions, site rules, restricted areas, and visitor-monitoring practices. Their experience is particularly relevant to identifying practical requirements for the system.
0241 [normal] Technological Experience: Novice (provisional design baseline). Their actual technological proficiency has not been established. The system should therefore allow common tasks such as ticket validation, walk-in registration, and attendance recording to be completed through simple and clearly presented steps.
0242 [normal] Other User Characteristics: Gatekeepers work directly at the tourism destinations and may perform several responsibilities while assisting visitors. The interviews also identified limited network reception as a concern at one of the destinations. Their interface should therefore prioritize simplicity, efficiency, and clear feedback during transactions. Possible connectivity limitations should also be considered when evaluating the system's operational requirements.
0243 [normal] 3. Visitors or Tourists
0244 [normal] User Category: Visitors/Tourists
0245 [normal] User Role: Visitors or tourists will use the public-facing features of the proposed system to view destination information, locate participating destinations through mapping features, check available schedules, make reservations, and obtain digital tickets. Walk-in visitors will be accommodated through registration by authorized site personnel.
0246 [normal] Subject Matter Experience: Novice. Visitors are not expected to have prior knowledge of the destinations' internal admission procedures, scheduling arrangements, or visitor-management practices.
0247 [normal] Technological Experience: Novice (provisional design baseline). Visitors may have different levels of experience with smartphones, websites, and online reservations. The system should remain understandable to users with basic technological knowledge.
0248 [normal] Other User Characteristics: Visitors may differ in age, place of residence, language, technological experience, and familiarity with the selected destinations. Some may be first-time visitors, while others may prefer walk-in admission rather than advance booking. The public interface should therefore provide clear instructions, readable destination information, mobile-friendly navigation, and an uncomplicated reservation process.

0260 [normal] Persona 1: Andre – Site Gatekeeper
0261 [normal] Maria is a 42-year-old site gatekeeper assigned to a nature-based tourist destination in Iligan City. She lives near the city and spends most of her working day assisting visitors, explaining site rules, monitoring entry, and recording visitor information. She is familiar with the site's operating procedures and prefers straightforward methods of completing her daily responsibilities.

0472 [normal] Time Slot: A defined period made available for visitor reservations or admission under the proposed scheduling process.
0473 [normal] Visitor Arrival Recording: The proposed process of documenting that a visitor has entered a destination.
0474 [normal] Visitor Departure Recording: The proposed process of documenting that a visitor has left a destination. Departure recording is intended to provide additional information for attendance and occupancy monitoring.
0475 [normal] Current Occupancy: The estimated number of visitors present within a destination at a particular time, based on recorded arrivals and departures. Its accuracy depends on the completeness of these records.
0476 [normal] Visitor Limit: The maximum number of visitors permitted under an established admission or scheduling rule. A proposed physical capacity estimate does not automatically become an officially approved visitor limit.
0477 [normal] Physical Carrying Capacity (PCC): An estimate of the number of visitors that can be physically accommodated based on usable visitor space and space allocation per visitor. When operating hours and average visit duration are considered, the calculation may also estimate the number of visitor admissions possible within an operating period. It does not independently establish ecological sustainability or safety.
0478 [normal] Usable Visitor Area: The portion of a destination available for the visitor activities being assessed, excluding areas that are restricted or unsuitable for the intended activity.
0479 [normal] Capacity Mapping: A proposed feature that visually represents identified usable visitor areas, restricted areas, and their corresponding physical capacity estimates to support visitor scheduling and management.
0480 [normal] Destination Mapping: A proposed feature that displays the geographic locations of participating tourism destinations to help visitors identify and locate them.

0857 [normal] 11a. Ease of Use Requirements
0858 [normal] Content
0859 [normal] This section describes your client’s aspirations for how easy it is for the intended users of the product to operate it. The product’s usability is derived from the abilities of the expected users of the product and the complexity of its functionality.
0860 [normal] The usability requirements should cover properties such as these:
0861 [normal] ● Efficiency of use: How quickly or accurately the user can use the product.
0862 [normal] ● Ease of remembering: How much the casual user is expected to remember about using the product.
0863 [normal] ● Error rates: For some products it is crucial that the user commits very few, or no, errors.
0864 [normal] ● Overall satisfaction in using the product: This is especially important for commercial, interactive products that face a lot of competition. Web sites are a good example.
0865 [normal] ● Feedback: How much feedback the user needs to feel confident that the product is actually accurately doing what the user expects. The necessary degree of feedback will be higher for some products (e.g., safety-critical products) than for others.

0870 [normal] The product shall help the user to avoid making mistakes.

0914 [normal] 11d. Understandability and Politeness Requirements This section is concerned with discovering requirements related to concepts and metaphors that are familiar to the intended end users.
0915 [normal] Content
0916 [normal] This specifies the requirement for the product to be understood by its users. 1367 [normal] 23. Risks
1368 [normal] All projects involve risk—namely, the risk that something will go wrong. Risk is not necessarily a bad thing, as no progress is made without taking some risk. Risk is only a bad thing if the risks are ignored and they become problems. Risk management entails assessing which risks are most likely to apply to the project, deciding a course of action if they become problems, and monitoring projects to give early warnings of risks becoming problems.
1369 [normal] Content
1370 [normal] This section of the specification contains a list of the most likely and the most serious risks for your project. For each risk, include the probability of it becoming a problem and any contingency plans.

1373 [normal] Considerations
1374 [normal] Risks will undoubtedly change during the lifetime of a project. The better you understand the requirements the better you can identify which risks are most serious for your project. The project manager determines how to manage the risks but the requirements specialists and developers provide input on new
1375 [normal] risks and which risks are turning into problems.
1376 [normal] Use your knowledge of the requirements as input to discover which risks are most relevant to your project. Use the Volere Requirements Knowledge Model (included with the download of Version 16 of the template) as a trigger for identifying relevant risks.
1377 [normal] It is also useful input to project management if you include the impact on the schedule, and/or the cost, if the risk does become a problem.
1378 [normal] As an alternative, you may prefer to identify the single largest risk—the showstopper. If this risk becomes a problem, then the project will definitely fail. Identifying a single risk in this way focuses attention on the single most

1396 [normal] The other cost of requirements is the amount of money or effort that you have to spend building them into a product. 1398 [normal] There is no best method to use when estimating. The important thing is to create your estimates using metrics directly related to the requirements. If you have specified the requirements in the way we have described, you will have the following metrics:
1399 [normal] ● Number of input and output flows on the work context ● Number of business events
1400 [normal] ● Number of product use cases
1401 [normal] ● Number of functional requirements
1402 [normal] ● Number of non-functional requirements
1403 [normal] ● Number of requirements constraints
1404 [normal] ● Number of function points
1405 [normal] The more detailed the work you do on your requirements, the more accurate your estimates will be. Your cost estimate is the amount of resources you estimate each type of deliverable will take to produce within your environment. You can create some very early cost estimates based on the work context. At that stage, your knowledge of the work will be general, and you should reflect this vagueness by making the cost estimate a range rather than a single figure. You can use these metrics as the basis for estimating the time, effort, and cost of building the product. First you need to determine what each of these metrics means within the environment in which you are building the product. For example, do you know how long it will take you to do all the work necessary to implement a product use case? If you do not, then you can take one of the use cases and benchmark it.
1406 [normal] As you increase your knowledge of the requirements, we suggest you try using function point counting—not because it is an inherently superior method, but because it is so widely accepted. So much is known about function point counting that it is possible to make easy comparisons with other products and other installations’ productivity. For details on how to estimate requirements effort and costs, refer to Mastering the Requirements Process, Addison Wesley, 2011. Appendix C Function Point Counting: A Simplified Introduction
1407 [normal] It is important that your client be told at this stage what the product is likely to cost. You usually express this amount as the total cost to complete the product, but you may also find it advantageous to point out the cost of the requirements effort, or the costs of individual requirements.
1408 [normal] Whatever you do, do not leave the costs in the lap of hysterical optimism. Make sure that this section includes meaningful numbers based on tangible deliverables.

1439 [normal] 26. Waiting Room
1440 [normal] The waiting room holds requirements that will not, for one reason or another, be part of the initial release of the product. If you are competent at gathering requirements, your users may often be inspired to think of more requirements than you can fit within the constraints of the project. While you may not want to include all of these requirements in the initial version of the product, neither do you want to lose them. If you are doing iterative development then the waiting room is your backlog.
1441 [normal] Content
1442 [normal] Any type of requirement at any level of detail.
1443 [normal] Copyright © the Atlantic Systems Guild Limited Volere Template V16 /78
1444 [normal] Motivation
1445 [normal] To allow requirements to be captured, even though they will not be part of the current development. To ensure that good ideas are not lost. To manage a backlog.
1446 [normal] Considerations
1447 [normal] The requirements-discovery process often throws up requirements that are beyond the sophistication of, or time allowed for, the current release of the product. This section holds these requirements in waiting. The intention is to avoid stifling the creativity of your users and clients, by using a repository to retain future requirements. You are also managing expectations by making it clear that you take these requirements seriously, although they will not be part of the agreed-upon product.
1448 [normal] Many people use the waiting room as a way of planning future versions of the product. Each requirement in the waiting room is tagged with its intended version number. As a requirement progresses closer to implementation, then you can spend more time on it and add details such as the cost and benefit attached to that requirement.
1449 [normal] You might also prioritize the contents of your waiting room. “Low-hanging fruit”—requirements that provide a high benefit at a low cost of
1450 [normal] implementation—are the highest-ranking candidates for the next release. You would also give a high waiting room rank to requirements for which there is a pent-up demand. You can think of the waiting room as a way of managing your backlog.
1451 [normal] The waiting room has a calming effect on everyone because it shows their ideas are being taken seriously. Your users and client know the requirements are not forgotten, merely parked until it is time to review them and make decisions about whether they will be incorporated in the product.
1452 [normal] 27. Ideas for Solutions
1453 [normal] Ideas for solutions are obviously not requirements, but it is impossible when gathering requirements not to get ideas for how they might be implemented. Rather than discarding the ideas or—even worse—writing them as if they are requirements, a practical idea is to simply set aside a section of your specification for these ideas. Record each one faithfully in this area, and then hand them over, along with the requirements, to your designer.

1455 [normal] Content
1456 [normal] Any idea for a solution that you think is worth keeping for future consideration. This can take the form of rough notes, sketches, pointers to other documents, pointers to people, pointers to existing products, prototypes and so on. The aim is to capture, with the least amount of effort, an idea that you can return to later.
1457 [normal] Motivation
1458 [normal] To make sure that good ideas are not lost. To help you separate requirements from solutions.
1459 [normal] Considerations
1460 [normal] While you are gathering requirements, you will inevitably have solution ideas; this section offers a way to capture them. Bear in mind that this section will not necessarily be included in every document that you publish.
